**Two people joined in the same week. One came back the following week. Both appeared the week after that.** A weekly active-user total hides this story; a cohort table makes it visible.

We will group people by signup week, count who returns at each age, and build a small retention heatmap. The blank cells are as important as the colored ones.

A cohort begins on Monday. “Active” means at least one recorded event of any type in that calendar week. Our observation cutoff is **January 26 at 00:00 UTC, exclusive**: the week beginning January 19 is complete; January 26 and later are not observed.

This is calendar-week retention, not rolling seven-day retention. A Sunday signup and a Monday signup can have very different elapsed times at their first week boundary. Choose the definition that fits the decision.

## Collapse repeat events before counting people

The setup creates `cohort_members` from signup dates and `active_weeks` from distinct user-week pairs. Inspect their relationship:

<TutorialExample example={demo.examples[0]} />

Ada’s many events in signup week contribute one active user-week. Her January 14 visit is week 1; her January 20 visit is week 2. Ben skips week 1 and returns in week 2.

`date_trunc('week', ...)` anchors the dates to Mondays. Calculating the week difference between these aligned boundaries produces the age of each cohort. Activity before a user’s signup cohort is excluded from the retention calculation.

## Build the cells that have no activity too

If we group only observed events, a week with no returns disappears. Instead, cross each cohort with the week numbers we want, then left join the observed counts:

<TutorialExample example={demo.examples[1]} />

The denominator comes from **all signups in the cohort**, not the people who happen to return. The January 5 cohort has two members at every age. One returning person in week 1 therefore means 50%, even though that week’s activity table contains only Ada.

`coalesce` supplies zero for an observed period with no activity. The outer `CASE` leaves unobserved future periods NULL. Here the cutoff is a Monday, so every included week is complete. For an arbitrary midweek cutoff, require the period’s end to be at or before the cutoff before treating it as a completed week.

The fixture assumes event collection is complete through the cutoff. A missing export is not evidence of zero retention.

## Turn the grid into a report

<TutorialExample example={demo.examples[2]} />

The January 5 cohort reads **100%, 50%, 100%**. Retention can rise again because this metric asks who was active in each particular week. It does not require uninterrupted weekly activity.

The January 12 cohort has 0% in week 1: that week was observed, and neither member returned. Its week 2 cell is NULL because it lies beyond the observation cutoff. Displaying both as zero would make newer cohorts look artificially worse.

## Make the cell inspectable

This tiny dataset is useful because we can name every person behind a percentage. Keep the same ability in a real analysis: a denominator, an observation window, and a query that explains membership.

Week 0 is not automatically 100% under this definition. It is 100% here because every signup also has a recorded event in that week. A signup with no event would remain in the denominator without contributing to activity. Decide whether that is the behavior your product team wants before interpreting the heatmap.
